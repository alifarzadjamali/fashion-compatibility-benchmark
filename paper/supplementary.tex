\section{POG category support and image inspection}\label{sec:pog-support}
Within each partition we count distinct image groups in each native catalog category and remove outfits in categories with insufficient support. We repeat the check because removing an outfit can also remove the last supporting image for another category. This supplies within-category distractors without reusing a correct image or drawing from another partition. POG clicks determine resampling groups only. Positive labels come from the recorded outfits.

Four inspected images from the largest POG group (209 identifiers) contained no visible product. Two were transparent and two were solid white. All four render white under the frozen preprocessing. POG has no stated licence. We distribute its identifiers and split files only.

\section{Dimensionality and supervision}\label{sec:probe-details}
With native features, the number of fitted coefficients varies with the encoder. The projection sensitivity instead fixes the feature width and learns principal components on unique training items before forming pairs. A low-variance item coordinate can still yield an informative pairwise difference or product. Compression can also suppress noisy pair weights when few training examples are available. The pilot used a common projected width as its primary readout.

Adding the sum of the two item vectors to their product and distance gives the classifier access to the outfit's mean item vector: the sum averaged over all pairs is proportional to that mean. XGBoost and the multilayer perceptron change the decision surface applied to these outfit features. All variants use the same frozen encoders.

\section{Feature and fit reproducibility}\label{sec:reproduction}
In the train/validation spot checks, bfloat16 and full-precision embeddings have mean cosine 0.99955 across nine equal-sized samples (minimum item cosine 0.95659). Repeating extraction under the recorded bfloat16 settings recovers the stored vectors closely. On POG, reducing the visual-token budget of the Qwen models changes the sampled item embeddings appreciably more than on Polyvore or IQON. The POG comparison thus describes Qwen under the benchmark's shared image budget. Increasing that budget would define a different accuracy and cost comparison.

Independent fits on the two machines chose the same native probes on validation. Matching selections also kept the inputs to the transformer and A100 comparisons fixed.

The Polyvore cross-machine check covers \resCrossMachineFiles{} probe runs. The median absolute CP-AUC difference between the x86 and ARM runs is $\resCrossMachineMedianAbsDaucMantissa\times10^{\resCrossMachineMedianAbsDaucExponent}$, with a maximum of \resCrossMachineMaxAbsDaucFixed{} on an MLP sensitivity run.
Rebuilding the pilot with the same procedure reproduces the pilot metrics within 0.001 AUC (v1's original split files were not retained).
