\begin{abstract}
This study provides a controlled measurement of representation quality for relational outfit compatibility. Seven released frozen visual representations are evaluated under one shared pairwise logistic protocol with train-only principal-component analysis. The evidence combines leakage-audited Polyvore-D-Clean, prospective replication on the frozen IQON3000-Clean protocol, and unchanged A100 human/expert-grounded questions. Across both corpora, modern contrastive vision--language model (VLM) representations form a consistently stronger compatibility tier than ResNet50 and generic DINOv3 for compatibility prediction and fill-in-the-blank completion. The broad hierarchy replicates across the two leakage-audited corpora and is corroborated at tier level by A100. The tier-level result persists after exclusion of the two checkpoints with the greatest fashion-data provenance uncertainty. Fashion specialization is checkpoint- and recipe-dependent, and exact-checkpoint LookBench comparisons demonstrate that retrieval rank is not a reliable compatibility-selection rule. The principal conclusion remains stable across construction seeds, feature dimensionality, nonlinear downstream learning, provenance restrictions, label availability, and dependence-aware sensitivity analyses. Calibration, efficiency, and provenance provide complementary model-selection criteria. RepBench-Fashion contributes controlled comparison, audited protocol construction, cross-dataset and external corroboration, statistical testing, and an inspectable reproducibility package for relational fashion vision.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Fashion recommendation includes several related but non-equivalent problems: retrieving an item similar to a query, predicting a user's preference, completing a partial outfit, and judging whether a set of garments is mutually compatible. Recent surveys describe a field that increasingly combines visual, textual, and behavioural signals, while also noting persistent fragmentation in datasets, tasks, and evaluation practice \cite{ding2024fashion,deldjoo2024review}. The distinction matters. Item retrieval asks for proximity to an anchor; outfit compatibility asks whether relations among heterogeneous items form a plausible whole. A representation that is excellent for finding visually similar products need not expose the cues required for colour coordination, formality, season, silhouette, or balance. Fashion therefore provides a concrete application domain for the broader computer-vision question of representation transfer under relational visual reasoning.

The standard Polyvore literature established compatibility prediction (CP) and fill-in-the-blank (FITB) completion as widely used evaluations \cite{han2017bilstm,vasileva2018typeaware,cucurull2019context}. It also encouraged increasingly expressive task-specific architectures: sequence models, type-conditioned metrics, graph reasoning, attention, set models, and transformers. Such work is essential, but it leaves a complementary empirical question under-measured. Given the rapid progression from supervised convolutional networks to self-supervised vision transformers and contrastive vision--language models (VLMs), how much compatibility information is already accessible in a frozen image representation when the downstream learner is held fixed?

Answering that question requires more than replacing one backbone in an existing pipeline. First, outfit datasets contain repeated items and duplicate images. Random or legacy splits can therefore allow the same product evidence to cross training and test boundaries, a concrete form of leakage \cite{kaufman2011leakage,kapoor2023leakage}. Second, synthetic negatives and FITB distractors are part of the measurement instrument: changing them can change both absolute scores and rankings. Third, fashion-specialized checkpoints may have incomplete data provenance, especially when their upstream data are web-scale or proprietary. Fourth, point estimates alone obscure paired uncertainty and the dependence induced by shared outfits, items, or users. Finally, predictive quality is only one deployment criterion; probability calibration and representation cost can favour different choices.

We make the released representation checkpoint the experimental unit. Seven frozen image representations receive exactly the same downstream transformation: a scaler and randomized principal-component analysis (PCA) fitted on training items only, an unordered pair construction, mean pooling over all item pairs, and a regularized logistic classifier. Holding the relational learner, labels, candidate sets, and selection logic fixed prevents representation choice from being confounded with simultaneous changes to architecture, modality, loss, personalization, or question construction. The roster spans an ImageNet-supervised ResNet50 \cite{deng2009imagenet,he2016resnet}; the self-supervised DINOv3 ViT-L/16 \cite{caron2021dino,oquab2024dinov2,simeoni2025dinov3}; generic CLIP and SigLIP2 VLMs \cite{radford2021clip,zhai2023siglip,tschannen2025siglip2}; FashionCLIP \cite{chia2022fashionclip}; and the fashion-retrieval-oriented Marqo-FashionSigLIP and GR-Lite \cite{zhu2025gcl,marqo2024fashionsiglip,gao2026lookbench}. The study's scope is controlled representation transfer for relational outfit compatibility.

The evidence is deliberately layered. The protocol audit detected cross-split item sharing and exact-image duplicates hidden behind different identifiers, rebuilt Polyvore by item/image-connected components, and confronted an IQON component containing 98.08\% of valid outfits. The IQON construction makes the trade-off between split integrity and source-population coverage explicit: the retained subset preserves nearly all users and the category mixture, while the audit identifies measurable shifts in outfit length and item repetition. Generated CP negatives and FITB distractors are checked for collisions, and a historical-to-clean question bridge separates the effect of regenerated questions from the effect of split cleaning. Polyvore-D-Clean provides development-influenced generalization evidence; the frozen IQON3000-Clean protocol, constructed prospectively from the larger IQON3000 corpus \cite{nakamura2018iqon}, provides replication within a second leakage-audited target. A100 adds unchanged external evidence grounded in expert and human judgments \cite{zou2022a100}; no A100 label or candidate enters training, calibration, or selection. Additional analyses cover an image-only OutfitTransformer adaptation \cite{sarkar2023outfittransformer}, five construction seeds, feature width, nonlinear downstream learning, compatibility-label efficiency, calibration, compute, checkpoint provenance, failure consensus, and exact-checkpoint LookBench retrieval ranks.

Four linked gaps motivate the study. \textit{G1}: contemporary frozen checkpoints have not been compared systematically for relational compatibility with downstream capacity and question construction held constant across leakage-audited corpora. \textit{G2}: the boundaries between frozen transfer, task-specific contextual learning, fashion specialization, retrieval quality, and external human/expert-grounded judgments remain poorly triangulated. \textit{G3}: compatibility leaderboards rarely consider benchmark calibration, computational cost, and checkpoint provenance together with discrimination. \textit{G4}: conclusions can be fragile to duplicate leakage, synthetic-question construction, retained-subset selection, dependence, feature width, learner choice, and labelled-outfit availability.

Accordingly, the four objectives are to (\textit{O1}) measure and prospectively replicate the shared-probe hierarchy; (\textit{O2}) delimit its construct and modelling boundaries; (\textit{O3}) compare operational model-selection axes; and (\textit{O4}) audit the hierarchy under plausible validity threats. The corresponding contributions are (\textit{C1}) a controlled seven-checkpoint benchmark whose family-level hierarchy replicates on the frozen IQON3000-Clean protocol; (\textit{C2}) external and construct-boundary triangulation through untouched A100 questions, a contextual baseline, six predefined specialization and selection contrasts, and exact-checkpoint retrieval results; (\textit{C3}) an operational evaluation framework combining discrimination, benchmark calibration, computational efficiency, provenance, and statistical uncertainty; and (\textit{C4}) leakage-audited Polyvore and IQON protocols plus a reproducible validity suite covering duplicate connectivity, generated-question collisions, the historical bridge, IQON retention and dependence, construction seeds, feature width, learner choice, compatibility-label availability, and frozen artifact traceability. Section~\ref{sec:design} formalizes the one-to-one mapping from these contributions to evidence and discussion answers.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Fashion compatibility, recommendation, and datasets}

Early image-based recommendation work learned notions of style and substitution from co-occurrence and preference data \cite{mcauley2015styles,veit2015style}. Polyvore-based research then formalized set-level compatibility and FITB completion. The bidirectional-LSTM formulation of Han et al. \cite{han2017bilstm} treated outfits as ordered sequences, while type-aware embeddings \cite{vasileva2018typeaware}, mixed-category metric learning \cite{chen2018dress}, and cross-category compatibility learning \cite{polania2019categories} made item type explicit. Context-aware models reason over an item relative to the remaining outfit \cite{cucurull2019context}; set-based models remove arbitrary sequence assumptions \cite{li2017set}; and attentive knowledge distillation transfers structure from more elaborate teachers \cite{song2018attentive}. OutfitTransformer provides a recent contextual set representation for recommendation and compatibility \cite{sarkar2023outfittransformer}.

Compatibility has also been approached through capsule wardrobes \cite{hsiao2018capsule}, personalized outfit generation \cite{chen2019pog}, personalized pairwise ranking \cite{song2019gpbpr}, and multi-layer comparison networks that diagnose incompatible pieces \cite{wang2019mcn}. Attribute-aware and explanatory methods seek reasons rather than only scores \cite{yang2019attributes,chen2019visualexplanations,lin2020explainable,balim2023diagnosing,wang2024pfe,li2024attribute}; a recent review organizes this explainable-compatibility literature \cite{selwon2024explainable}. Self-supervised visual attribute learning further shows that compatibility-relevant signals can be shaped without direct labels \cite{kim2021sval}. These approaches vary in modality, personalization, objective, and downstream capacity. Our goal is different: hold the compatibility learner constant and measure the representation supplied to it.

Polyvore's catalog-like images and repeated products made it influential for controlled compatibility tasks, while IQON3000 added a larger user-curated collection with user and stylistic context \cite{nakamura2018iqon}. Broader fashion datasets such as DeepFashion support recognition and retrieval rather than outfit compatibility per se \cite{liu2016deepfashion}; Fashion200K supports language-aware fashion concepts \cite{han2017spatial}; and Street2Shop targets cross-domain product retrieval \cite{kiapour2015street}. These task differences motivate caution when transferring conclusions from item retrieval to relational compatibility.

A100 was introduced specifically to assess aesthetic ability beyond synthetic Polyvore evaluation \cite{zou2022a100}. Its released five-choice \emph{Liberalism Aesthetic Test} (LAT) and \emph{Academicism Aesthetic Test} (AAT) probe whole-outfit aesthetic choice; AAT records are grouped by colour, style, occasion, season, material, and balance. These tasks provide valuable human/expert grounding, so we use them as external corroboration and report uncertainty, including sensitivity to two released LAT label interpretations. A subsequent higher-order network study uses A100 within a task-specific compatibility and cross-selling system \cite{pang2026crossselling}.

\subsection{Generic and domain-specialized visual representations}

The transferability of representations is not guaranteed by source-task accuracy. ImageNet model quality often correlates with transfer, but task and protocol can alter the relationship \cite{kornblith2019transfer}. Similar variation occurs among self-supervised objectives \cite{ericsson2021transfer}. Broad suites such as VTAB, ELEVATER, and WILDS expose the importance of multiple target tasks and distribution shift \cite{zhai2019vtab,li2022elevater,koh2021wilds}. Recent e-commerce embedding comparisons likewise evaluate foundation-model transfer, tuning, retrieval, classification, and practical cost \cite{czerwinska2025ecommerce}. In applied computer vision, systematic comparison of large and small MLLMs across capability categories and application settings has shown how benchmark design can expose capability boundaries and deployment trade-offs without making architecture invention the experimental question \cite{feng2025mllmbenchmark}. RepBench-Fashion brings this controlled-measurement perspective to a relational visual target.

Vision Transformers \cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} enabled scalable visual encoders, while CLIP aligned image and language representations through contrastive web supervision \cite{radford2021clip}. ALIGN demonstrated similar scaling with noisy image--text data \cite{jia2021align}; LAION-5B later provided an open web-scale resource for such training \cite{schuhmann2022laion}. SigLIP replaced global softmax normalization with independent sigmoid losses \cite{zhai2023siglip}, and SigLIP2 combined that foundation with multilingual, self-supervised, captioning, and curation objectives \cite{tschannen2025siglip2}. In parallel, DINO and DINOv2 established strong label-free ViT features \cite{caron2021dino,oquab2024dinov2}; DINOv3 scaled this family and introduced mechanisms for stable dense representations \cite{simeoni2025dinov3}.

Fashion-specific adaptation can encode product vocabulary and domain cues. FashionCLIP fine-tunes a CLIP-style model on more than 800,000 Farfetch product image--text pairs and reports gains on fashion retrieval, classification, and grounding \cite{chia2022fashionclip}. Generalized Contrastive Learning incorporates graded rankings into a retrieval representation \cite{zhu2025gcl}; Marqo-FashionSigLIP is a released fashion checkpoint associated with that ecosystem \cite{marqo2024fashionsiglip}. GR-Lite is the open fashion retrieval model released with LookBench \cite{gao2026lookbench}. Recent retrieval audits and specialization efforts further show that benchmark composition and fine-tuning data affect fashion retrieval conclusions \cite{czerwinska2025ecommerce,xue2026zooclaw}. Domain specialization might help compatibility, but it might instead emphasize item similarity or reflect a particular training recipe. Direct controlled comparison is therefore necessary.

\subsection{Closest contemporary systems and scope}

Recent work defines the closest boundary to our contribution. VICTOR combines fashion-specific contrastive pretraining with a multitask Transformer for incompatibility detection \cite{papadopoulos2023victor}; OutfitTransformer learns contextual set representations \cite{sarkar2023outfittransformer}; HAT incorporates personal history \cite{jung2025hat}; and MEDAL learns task-specific distilled embedding spaces \cite{sanny2026medal}. Text2Outfit studies multimodal large-model outfit generation \cite{zhai2025text2outfit}, while recent systems fine-tune multimodal models or pretrain cross-item relations for product bundling \cite{liu2025bundlemllm,ma2024cirp}. LMLMO combines generated fashion cues with BEiT3 compatibility representations \cite{chang2025large}, and FashionStylist adds expert-knowledge-enhanced data and multimodal styling evaluations \cite{feng2026fashionstylist}. These are complementary end-to-end or task-specific contributions, not interchangeable frozen-checkpoint measurements.

\subsection{Calibration, efficiency, and reproducible comparison}

ROC--AUC measures discrimination, not whether predicted probabilities correspond to empirical frequencies. The Brier score is a proper squared-probability loss \cite{brier1950}; log loss offers a complementary proper scoring rule; and expected calibration error (ECE) summarizes reliability-bin discrepancies, although its value depends on binning. Calibration studies show that modern neural systems can be accurate yet miscalibrated and that calibration assessment itself requires care \cite{niculescu2005probabilities,guo2017calibration,naeini2015calibration,vaicenavicius2019calibration,minderer2021revisiting}. Under distribution shift, uncertainty can degrade independently of accuracy \cite{ovadia2019uncertainty}. We consequently report discrimination, proper scores, and ECE sensitivity rather than collapsing them into one ranking.

Efficiency has analogous measurement problems. Parameter count, operations, latency, throughput, memory, and end-to-end extraction time answer different questions; ``efficient'' without a declared measure is underspecified \cite{dehghani2022efficiency}. Hardware and software stacks affect results \cite{hooker2021hardware}, while systematic compute and carbon reporting improves accountability \cite{schwartz2020green,henderson2020reporting,dodge2022carbon}. We report same-device measurements and Pareto frontiers, but avoid extrapolating a single GPU result to all deployments.

Finally, model comparisons require paired analysis and disciplined multiplicity. Bootstrap methods provide distribution-light interval estimates \cite{efron1994bootstrap}; repeated training and benchmark construction introduce variance that should be exposed rather than hidden \cite{bouthillier2021variance}. Multiple-dataset comparisons and resampled estimates require care because observations and folds are dependent \cite{demsar2006statistical,nadeau2003inference}. We use group-aware paired resampling, exact McNemar tests for paired FITB decisions where applicable, and Holm's sequential correction for predefined contrast families \cite{holm1979}. Protocol hashes, manifests, and audit artifacts follow reproducibility recommendations to make the reported result traceable \cite{pineau2021reproducibility}.

\section{Research questions and study design}\label{sec:design}

The benchmark was frozen before final IQON and A100 evaluation. Four questions provide a one-to-one path from gap to objective, contribution, evidence, and answer.

\begin{description}
\item[RQ1:] Under an identical frozen-feature relational probe, how do the seven released checkpoints compare on item- and exact-image-disjoint Polyvore, and does the broad hierarchy prospectively replicate on IQON3000-Clean?
\item[RQ2:] How far do those conclusions extend to A100 human/expert-grounded questions, task-specific contextual learning, fashion-specialization contrasts, and retrieval-versus-compatibility ranking?
\item[RQ3:] Do discrimination, benchmark calibration, measured efficiency, and checkpoint provenance favour the same representation?
\item[RQ4:] Which conclusions survive audits of leakage and question validity, historical question regeneration, IQON retention and dependence, construction seeds, feature width, learner choice, compatibility-label availability, outfit length, and failure consensus?
\end{description}

The experimental unit is a frozen representation checkpoint, not an entire end-to-end recommendation system. Images are encoded once. All representations receive the same downstream feature construction, training labels, candidate sets, and validation criterion. Hyperparameters are selected separately per representation using validation CP ROC--AUC, never test or A100 outcomes. Polyvore-D-Clean influenced development choices; its results therefore establish controlled in-domain evidence, not a prospective blind test. The IQON protocol and evaluation path were frozen subsequently and are treated as replication. This asymmetry is maintained throughout the paper rather than blending both datasets into a single discovery analysis.

Six contrasts were specified before final test evaluation, each tied to a distinct scientific or model-selection question:
\begin{enumerate}
\item DINOv3 versus ResNet50 tests generic modern self-supervision against the conventional supervised CNN baseline.
\item GR-Lite versus DINOv3 tests fashion-retrieval-oriented specialization relative to the generic DINO-family reference.
\item FashionCLIP versus CLIP tests fashion-domain specialization relative to its generic contrastive VLM family.
\item Marqo-FashionSigLIP versus SigLIP2 tests fashion-oriented specialization relative to a generic sigmoid-loss VLM family.
\item The validation-selected best fashion-specialized versus validation-selected best generic checkpoint tests the practical family-level question of whether specialization improves checkpoint selection.
\item The validation-selected best overall checkpoint versus ResNet50 quantifies the practical gain over the conventional baseline.
\end{enumerate}
Final test outcomes were not used to select arbitrary pairwise comparisons. Holm correction controls this predefined contrast family within each dataset--metric family. We intentionally avoid an all-pairs significance matrix, which would dilute these targeted hypotheses and encourage post hoc interpretation. Retention, item-component, and collision analyses added after the audit are explicitly labelled as post-audit sensitivity analyses rather than predefined hypothesis tests.

Table~\ref{tab:datasets} summarizes the four protocols. CP is a balanced binary task: each observed outfit is a positive, and its matched negative replaces \emph{every} item with a different same-category item from the same split while retaining outfit length and category sequence. FITB supplies one partial outfit and four candidates, exactly one of which is the held-out item; the remaining three are distinct same-category, within-split distractors. Candidate positions are balanced. These controlled corruptions reduce category and length shortcuts but remain synthetic and should not be interpreted as the population distribution of incompatible outfits.

Question construction explicitly rejects any CP item set already observed as a positive, duplicate generated negatives, any FITB distractor completion already observed as a positive, and duplicated candidates within a question. A frozen-manifest audit additionally found no candidate duplication by exact decoded pixels. Across all six clean splits, the audit found zero CP positive collisions by item or exact-image set and zero FITB positive-completion collisions; the largest cross-question reuse count for a distractor was one on Polyvore and two on IQON. Cross-question reuse is allowed, but no within-question duplication is. Section~\ref{sec:protocolaudit} and the supplement report these validation counts.

\subsection{Polyvore-D-Clean}

Starting from the pooled historical Polyvore-D positives, eight exact duplicate outfits were removed. We formed a graph whose nodes were outfits and whose edges indicated a shared item identifier, an identical encoded image, or identical decoded pixels. There were 120 exact-image duplicate groups. Connected components, rather than individual outfits, were assigned deterministically to train, validation, or test, targeting the historical split sizes. The final split contains 16,991/2,999/15,142 positive outfits and 68,166/13,780/70,839 unique items. Audits report zero cross-split item-identifier, encoded-file, decoded-pixel, or outfit overlap.

Negatives and FITB candidates were then reconstructed inside each split using category and outfit-length constraints. This order is important: generating corruptions before splitting could reintroduce cross-split evidence. Historical Polyvore-D retains its original 16,995/3,000/15,145 outfits and packaged candidates solely for comparison with prior work. It is not used to support leakage-audited claims.

\subsection{IQON3000-Clean}

The source archive contains 308,747 JSON records. After requiring at least two distinct items, 297,916 valid outfits from 3,568 users and 712,170 unique source items remained; 610 duplicate outfits were detected. Direct component splitting was infeasible because one item/image-connected component contained 292,197 outfits (98.08\% of the valid corpus). We therefore used deterministic balanced greedy hypergraph packing. Item identifiers and exact encoded/decoded image groups were indivisible; outfits that would connect split boundaries were discarded. Exact targets of 86,800/12,400/24,800 positive outfits were met, retaining 41.62\% of valid source outfits. The test set yields 49,600 CP examples and 24,800 FITB questions.

The retained protocol has zero cross-split identifier, exact encoded-image, decoded-pixel, and outfit overlap. It is not user-disjoint: the giant connectivity structure and retention target make item/image disjointness the enforced constraint, while users may occur in multiple splits. The retained-versus-excluded audit preserves 98.74\% of source users and finds very small category-distribution divergence (Jensen--Shannon divergence 0.0016 bits). It also measures the selection effect: retained outfits are shorter (mean 5.28 versus 6.47 items) and contain less frequently repeated items (mean source frequency 8.73 versus 13.44). Reporting both preservation and shift makes the trade-off between split integrity and source-population coverage explicit without treating the subset as representative of every valid source outfit. IQON confidence intervals primarily resample user clusters; an item-connected-component sensitivity is added because user clustering does not capture all graph dependence. IQON3000-Clean is a prospective replication within the leakage-audited IQON target, not a new-user generalization benchmark.

\subsection{A100 external evaluation}

A100 is left unchanged and evaluated exactly as its released five-choice records: the probe scores the completed outfit produced by appending each candidate to the supplied partial outfit and selects the highest score. LAT contains 847 image-reference instances resolving to 680 referenced files and 680 unique exact images; AAT contains 983 instances resolving to 983 files and 977 unique exact images. Sixty-five exact images occur in both tasks, giving 1,663 referenced files but 1,592 globally unique exact images. None overlaps either clean training corpus by encoded bytes or decoded pixels. For LAT, the primary target is the majority label $\arg\max(\texttt{gt\_distribution})$; mean LAT (mLAT) credits the selected option's vote share. The archive's separate \texttt{gt} field disagrees on questions 92, 93, 94, 96, 98, 99, and 100, so archive-gt accuracy is a sensitivity analysis. AAT questions are partitioned as colour (1--20), style (21--52), occasion (53--67), season (68--79), material (80--91), and balance (92--100). Nothing is fitted, tuned, calibrated, or selected on A100.

Table~\ref{tab:representations} defines the roster. Each checkpoint is image-only at evaluation time; text is used only historically in VLM pretraining. Official preprocessing is retained, including resolution, interpolation, and normalization. ResNet50 uses the ImageNet-1K V2 weights and a 2,048-dimensional pooled feature. DINOv3 uses the normalized ViT-L/16 class-token representation. CLIP and SigLIP2 use their projected image embeddings. FashionCLIP uses its 512-dimensional projected embedding. Marqo-FashionSigLIP uses the normalized OpenCLIP image output, and GR-Lite uses its released 1,024-dimensional normalized class-token output.

The roster was selected to represent distinct pretraining regimes rather than a sequence of parameter-matched ablations. Parameter count and resolution consequently differ. This is intentional for model-choice measurement, but it prevents causal attribution of a result to one factor such as loss, data, or architecture. Exact revisions, preprocessing, licenses, and provenance notes are recorded because checkpoint identity is part of the intervention.

For an outfit $O=\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}$, a frozen encoder gives $e_i=f(x_i)$. A scaler and randomized PCA are fitted only to distinct training-item embeddings and applied to validation and test items. Unless otherwise stated, PCA retains 256 dimensions and the result is $\ell_2$ normalized to $z_i$. Each unordered item pair is represented as
\begin{equation}
q_{ij}=\left[|z_i-z_j|\,;\, z_i\odot z_j\right], \qquad i<j,
\label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
and outfit features are their mean,
\begin{equation}
\phi(O)=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}.
\label{eq:outfit}
\end{equation}
This 512-dimensional construction is invariant to item order and gives every pair equal weight. A second StandardScaler is fitted to training CP examples only. Logistic regression predicts
\begin{equation}
p(y=1\mid O)=\sigma(w^{\top}\tilde\phi(O)+b).
\end{equation}
The fixed-capacity learner exposes coordinate-wise distance and agreement while keeping the visual encoder and relational evaluation protocol constant across representations.

\section{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}

The logistic classifier uses L-BFGS for at most 2,000 iterations, no class weighting, and $C\in\{0.01,0.1,1,10,100,1000\}$. Validation CP ROC--AUC is the sole selection criterion; the classification threshold is 0.5. CP reports ROC--AUC as the primary metric, with PR--AUC, accuracy, balanced accuracy, Brier score, log loss, and equal-width ECE. FITB selects the candidate producing the highest completed-outfit compatibility score and reports accuracy; its scores are not interpreted as normalized candidate probabilities.

Uncertainty uses 2,000 paired bootstrap replicates. Polyvore resamples connected-component groups; IQON primarily resamples users; A100 resamples questions. Because IQON test outfits remain dependent through an item graph, a conservative sensitivity instead resamples its 9,231 test item-connected components; the largest contains 11,547 of 24,800 positive outfits. The six predefined contrasts are corrected by Holm's method within each dataset--metric family. CP interval estimates are paired and group-aware. FITB paired differences additionally use exact McNemar tests where the protocol permits, with bootstrap intervals for effect size. Rank replication is summarized by Spearman's $\rho$ and Kendall's $\tau$, treated descriptively given seven models.

Robustness checks were frozen alongside the main analysis, except the explicitly labelled post-audit IQON retention, item-component bootstrap, and question-collision diagnostics added in response to validity review. Construction seeds 42--46 hold item partitions fixed but regenerate same-category, within-split CP negatives and FITB distractors, then refit affected scalers and classifiers. Together with the collision audit and historical-to-clean question bridge, this tests whether the conclusion depends on one generated-question seed, exact false-negative collisions, or the change from packaged to reconstructed questions. Representation width is tested by replacing PCA-256 with native-dimensional features. Learner dependence is tested on IQON using XGBoost over the same outfit features. The compatibility-label analysis uses deterministic 10\%, 25\%, 50\%, and 100\% subsets of labelled training outfits; at every fraction, unsupervised PCA still has access to embeddings for all train-split items, while the outfit-feature scaler and classifier are refitted only on the selected labelled outfits. This measures labelled-outfit demand without changing the frozen encoder. Calibration uses 10, 15, and 20 equal-width bins. The provenance-restricted sensitivity removes Marqo-FashionSigLIP and GR-Lite, the two checkpoints with the greatest fashion-data provenance uncertainty. Failure and outfit-length analyses are descriptive.

The image-only OutfitTransformer adaptation projects cached ResNet50 features to 64 dimensions, prepends an outfit token, and applies six Transformer layers with 16 heads, a 256-unit feed-forward block, dropout 0.1, and no positional encoding. A two-layer binary head is optimized with focal binary cross-entropy ($\gamma=2$, $\alpha=0.25$), AdamW (learning rate $10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), batch size 50, for at most 30 epochs with validation-AUC early stopping (patience 5). Five training seeds (42--46) are reported. This tests whether task-specific contextual learning from a conventional visual backbone can compensate for weaker frozen item features. It is an adaptation to the image-only protocol, not an exact reproduction of the canonical multimodal OutfitTransformer.

Efficiency measurements use one NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti (16 GB). Repeated batch-64 inference follows two warm-up and five measured repeats; PyTorch FlopCounterMode estimates operations. End-to-end IQON extraction uses batch 32 across 434,089 retained item images. PCA and classifier timings are reported separately. Hardware-specific values support within-device comparisons only.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Protocol and construction audit (RQ4)}\label{sec:protocolaudit}

Exact split audits found no cross-split item identifier, encoded-file, decoded-pixel, or outfit overlap in either clean corpus. The construction audit described in Section~\ref{sec:datasets} also found no exact positive--negative collision or duplicated FITB candidate. These checks establish the audited item-, exact-image-, outfit-, and generated-question controls; their scope is defined in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

The IQON retention audit shows the cost of this guarantee. The protocol retains 41.62\% of valid outfits while preserving almost all users and the category mixture, and it identifies that retained outfits are shorter and contain less-repeated items. Moreover, 46.56\% of retained test positives remain in one test item-connected component. Primary user-cluster intervals therefore cannot eliminate all item-graph dependence. Component-cluster sensitivity is deliberately conservative: all six predefined IQON contrast directions remain unchanged, including DINOv3--ResNet50 CP 95\% CI [0.0517, 0.0651], Marqo--CLIP [0.0140, 0.0237], and the small adverse FashionCLIP--CLIP contrast [$-0.0105$, $-0.0003$]. This supports the reported hierarchy while leaving retained-subset generalization as a bounded limitation.

\subsection{Prospective replication on frozen IQON3000-Clean (RQ1)}\label{sec:iqon}

Absolute performance falls on IQON, consistent with a more heterogeneous user-curated corpus and a different negative/distractor population. The separation nonetheless persists: CLIP, SigLIP2, and FashionCLIP all remain clearly above generic DINOv3, which in turn exceeds ResNet50 by 0.0566 AUC (95\% CI 0.0508--0.0626) and 0.0494 FITB (0.0416--0.0580). GR-Lite exceeds DINOv3 by 0.0092 AUC (0.0026--0.0155) and 0.0172 FITB (0.0092--0.0255). Each predefined contrast remains supported after correction.

Within the full roster, Marqo-FashionSigLIP has the best point estimate for both tasks: 0.7221 AUC and 0.4451 FITB. It improves over SigLIP2 by 0.0190 AUC (0.0145--0.0234) and 0.0121 FITB (0.0050--0.0190), unlike the null Polyvore contrast. Against CLIP, the best specialized model adds 0.0179 AUC and 0.0087 FITB. FashionCLIP again trails CLIP, by 0.0062 AUC and 0.0089 FITB. The replication therefore sharpens the recipe-dependent conclusion: specialization depends on the released checkpoint and training recipe. Marqo's IQON lead is a checkpoint-specific result with additional provenance uncertainty; the replicated VLM-tier advantage does not rely on it.

\subsection{Task-specific contextual baseline (RQ2)}\label{sec:baseline}

The image-only OutfitTransformer adaptation reaches $0.8095\pm0.0020$ CP AUC and $0.4655\pm0.0051$ FITB on Polyvore, versus $0.7723\pm0.0013$ and $0.4354\pm0.0012$ on IQON (mean $\pm$ training-seed SD). Its IQON CP result exceeds every frozen-probe result, showing that supervised contextual learning can compensate for a conventional ResNet input when sufficient in-domain labels are available. On IQON FITB, however, it is approximately tied with CLIP and below Marqo's 0.4451. On Polyvore it trails all VLM probes on both tasks and trails DINOv3 on FITB.

External transfer is also mixed. Polyvore-trained baseline means are $0.490\pm0.051$ LAT and $0.340\pm0.029$ AAT; IQON-trained means are $0.418\pm0.026$ and $0.382\pm0.054$. The baseline is thus competitive for IQON CP but does not dominate across task, dataset, or external metric. Because its visual input, optimization, and capacity differ from the frozen probes, this comparison is diagnostic rather than a controlled representation contrast. It is an image-only contextual adaptation, not the canonical published implementation. Published VICTOR, HAT, MEDAL, HON, Text2Outfit, and multimodal results cannot be inserted as like-for-like baselines because their splits, modalities, personalization targets, negatives, or evaluation candidates differ.

Figure~\ref{fig:calibration} shows the reliability curves. The discrimination winner is not the calibration winner. Marqo has the lowest Brier score on both Polyvore (0.1322) and IQON (0.2197), consistent with its strong ranking and probability accuracy. ECE$_{15}$, however, is lowest for ResNet50: 0.0288 on Polyvore and 0.0415 on IQON. Marqo records 0.0350 and 0.0583, and FashionCLIP has the largest IQON ECE (0.0571 apart from Marqo's 0.0583). All three probability summaries describe \emph{benchmark calibration} under balanced synthetic CP, not calibration to the unknown real-world prevalence of compatible outfits. We therefore report them jointly and would fit any deployment recalibration on held-out deployment-like data. FITB scores are rankings, not calibrated probabilities.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Answer to RQ1: controlled transfer and replication}

Our results establish a stable tier-level advantage for the evaluated contrastive VLM representations over ResNet50 and generic DINOv3 under the shared relational probe. The broad ordering replicates prospectively on the frozen IQON3000-Clean protocol despite lower absolute scores: cross-dataset rank correlations are 0.964 for CP and 0.857 for FITB. Removing Marqo-FashionSigLIP and GR-Lite leaves the same family-level conclusion. Because the released checkpoints differ as complete packages, the comparison directly supports checkpoint selection, while the matched specialization contrasts distinguish family-level strength from recipe-specific gains.

\subsection{Answer to RQ2: construct and modelling boundaries}

The hierarchy extends beyond the synthetic clean questions at tier level. Unchanged A100 questions broadly corroborate the evaluated VLM advantage, while their sample size precludes reliable ordering of close VLMs. The contextual baseline wins IQON CP yet not every dataset, task, or external metric, showing that supervised contextual learning is competitive rather than uniformly dominant. Fashion specialization is checkpoint- and recipe-dependent: FashionCLIP trails CLIP, whereas Marqo improves on IQON. Exact-overlap LookBench rank reversals further establish that a fashion-retrieval leaderboard is not a reliable compatibility-selection rule for the matched checkpoints.

\subsection{Answer to RQ3: operational selection axes}

The axes do not agree. Marqo leads IQON discrimination and Brier score and is efficient on the measured GPU, but its fashion-data provenance is less certain and it does not minimize ECE. ResNet50 has the lowest benchmark ECE and low cost but weak discrimination. CLIP is predictively strong and computationally expensive; FashionCLIP is compact but does not lead compatibility. These findings concern balanced-protocol calibration and one hardware/software stack. Model choice should therefore state its error, probability, latency, memory, reproducibility, and data-governance constraints rather than collapse them into one leaderboard.

\subsection{Answer to RQ4: validity and robustness}

The main family hierarchy survives exact-collision audits, five regenerated question seeds, native-width features, XGBoost, uncertain-provenance exclusion, compatibility-label fractions, and conservative IQON item-component resampling. The historical bridge shows that apparent historical-to-clean score increases chiefly reflect regenerated questions rather than split cleaning. The IQON construction makes the trade-off between split integrity and source-population coverage measurable: 98.74\% of source users remain represented and category divergence is 0.0016 bits, while retained outfits are shorter and use less frequently repeated items. Predefined contrast directions are preserved under conservative item-component resampling. Together, these checks show that the principal conclusion is stable across the tested construction, dimensionality, learner, label, provenance, and dependence alternatives.

\subsection{Practical implications for machine-vision systems}

The results support direct decisions for fashion-vision research and deployment. An encoder should be selected on relational compatibility evidence rather than a fashion-retrieval leaderboard: the exact LookBench overlap contains consequential rank reversals. Fashion-domain pretraining is likewise not a sufficient selection criterion, because its effect depends on the released recipe. Strong frozen contrastive VLM features can support competitive compatibility prediction without encoder fine-tuning, making frozen transfer a practical baseline when training cost, update frequency, or data access constrain end-to-end adaptation.

Accuracy should not be the sole operational criterion. Benchmark calibration, latency, throughput, memory, and training-data provenance change the preferred checkpoint, and these constraints should be reported alongside discrimination. At the benchmark level, cross-split item identifiers and exact images must be audited together, connected duplicate structure must be respected during splitting, and generated CP negatives and FITB distractors must be checked against known positives and within-question duplication. These practices make machine-vision comparisons more interpretable and reduce the risk that an apparent model gain is instead a property of data leakage or question construction.

\section{Threats to validity and limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\textit{Construct validity.} CP negatives and FITB distractors are synthetic, although they are same-category, within-split, deterministically generated, collision-audited, and tested across five construction seeds. They approximate plausible confusions rather than naturally occurring disliked outfits; harder similarity-mined negatives remain a useful extension. Compatibility itself is culturally and contextually dependent, and image-only probes omit personal preference, garment fit, price, brand, and text. A100 improves human grounding but remains small. Its expert/human labels and facet partitions do not exhaust aesthetic judgments.

\textit{Internal validity.} Exact identifiers, encoded files, decoded pixels, and duplicate outfits are controlled across the leakage-audited splits, and generated questions have no audited exact positive collisions. Transformed or perceptually near-duplicate images cannot be exhaustively excluded. IQON hypergraph packing retains 41.62\% of valid outfits; it preserves nearly all users and the category mixture but shifts outfit length and item repetition, so it is not assumed to represent every valid source outfit. IQON is item/image-disjoint but not user-disjoint and therefore is not a new-user generalization benchmark. User-cluster bootstrap addresses one dependence source; item-component sensitivity preserves the main contrasts but is dominated by one large component.

\textit{Representation attribution and upstream provenance.} The seven checkpoints differ in model size, input resolution, architecture, data, objective, and post-processing, so the results compare released representations as packages rather than causally isolating one pretraining factor. The split audits do not establish whether CLIP, SigLIP2, DINOv3, FashionCLIP, Marqo-FashionSigLIP, GR-Lite, or visually related upstream content ever encountered Polyvore or IQON during web-scale pretraining. Marqo-FashionSigLIP and GR-Lite carry the greatest additional uncertainty because their fashion data are not item-wise disclosed. Excluding both preserves the tier-level result but does not certify the remaining web-pretrained models.

\textit{External validity.} Two clean fashion corpora and A100 do not cover all cultures, markets, product photography, or temporal trends. Historical Polyvore is included only for comparison. The LookBench analysis contains exactly five overlapping models and mixes an external retrieval metric with compatibility metrics; its correlations are descriptive. The efficiency study uses one recent consumer GPU, one software stack, fixed batch settings, and estimated FLOPs. Other accelerators, quantization, compilation, or serving loads could alter the frontier.

\textit{Probe and baseline validity.} The task-specific baseline is an image-only contextual adaptation built from the published OutfitTransformer design rather than an exact reproduction of its canonical multimodal training. Native-width and XGBoost sensitivity analyses cover plausible downstream alternatives but do not exhaust all possible relational probes.

\textit{Statistical validity.} Group-aware paired bootstrap preserves important dependence, and Holm correction limits the predefined family, but confidence intervals still inherit assumptions about the resampling unit. Construction-seed analysis covers five seeds; A100 intervals are wide; rank correlations over seven or five systems have low resolution. The retained-subset and collision diagnostics were post-audit validity analyses, not preregistered tests. Null or overlapping results are reported as inconclusive rather than evidence of equivalence. CP calibration is conditional on the constructed 50:50 protocol and cannot be transported to real-world prevalence without new calibration data.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

RepBench-Fashion provides a controlled measurement of representation quality for relational outfit compatibility. Modern contrastive VLM representations form a consistently stronger compatibility tier than ResNet50 and generic DINOv3; that family-level hierarchy replicates across the two leakage-audited corpora, is corroborated by A100, and persists under the provenance-restricted analysis. Fashion specialization remains checkpoint- and recipe-dependent, while retrieval ranks do not provide a reliable compatibility-selection rule. Calibration, efficiency, and provenance identify complementary deployment trade-offs. The historical bridge, collision audits, five construction seeds, feature-width and nonlinear-learner checks, label-fraction analysis, and dependence-aware sensitivity together support the stability of the principal conclusion. The public code, configuration, manifests, audit outputs, and frozen result summaries provide an inspectable basis for reproducing and extending relational fashion-representation evaluation.
